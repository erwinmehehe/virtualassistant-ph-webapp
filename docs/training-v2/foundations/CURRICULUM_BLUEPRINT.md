# Virtual Assistant Foundations V2 — Complete Curriculum Blueprint

**Version:** proposal 1.1 (20 October 2026)  
**Product:** VirtualAssistant.com.ph (VAPH) self-paced training  
**Learning level:** absolute beginner to supervised junior VA practice  
**Nominal duration:** **35 hours 15 minutes**, excluding optional coaching, repeat attempts and real-world on-the-job supervision  
**Scope:** complete instructional design specification, **not** proof of accreditation or production publication.

## Decision in one paragraph

Rebuild Foundations as a **practice-first, evidence-based course**. A lesson must teach a beginner what to do, show the process using a worked fictional client example, allow the learner to do the work with an actual file or simulated tool, and produce an artifact that can be checked against explicit criteria. A short article plus an 80-character reflection and multiple-choice final is not enough to demonstrate job competence. The V2 credential must distinguish what was *read and understood* from what was *performed and reviewed*.

### Learner and course assumptions

- A learner may have basic English reading ability and ordinary mobile/computer access but no previous VA employment.
- Primary examples fit a Philippines-based learner supporting an international client, including Australian business contexts where time zone, spelling, local terminology or privacy rules matter.
- Learners must be able to complete core practice using free or demo resources; don't require a paid subscription, live customer system, private client account, or personal information.
- A clear boundary runs through all lessons: administrative assistance does not confer authority over payments, refunds, contracts, security incidents, healthcare decisions, tax, regulated accounting or legal advice.
- Show both Gmail/Google Workspace and Outlook/Microsoft 365 patterns when teaching tools. UI walkthroughs must be checked against current interfaces before they become production screenshots.
- Avoid simulated employment claims, fabricated metrics, external AI handling of real client materials, and any suggestion that training guarantees work.

## What a successful learner can demonstrate

By the end, with an approved fictional brief and normal reference materials, the learner can:

1. Convert a vague request into a task, decision-rights map, acceptance criteria and documented questions.
2. Produce clear emails, client status updates, issue escalations and end-of-shift handoffs with owners and deadlines.
3. Triage a 12-item inbox, identify security/finance/customer risks, track next actions and refrain from making unauthorized promises.
4. Schedule meetings across time zones using a verified calendar date and zone, while documenting ambiguity and approval boundaries.
5. Organize files with consistent names, version history and least-privilege sharing rather than deleting uncertain source files.
6. Prepare a spreadsheet, validate data, spot duplicates/conflicts and explain what must be reviewed before any destructive change.
7. Maintain a task board with priorities, dependencies, acceptance criteria, escalation triggers and evidence of completion.
8. Compare sources, separate facts from assumptions and prepare a concise recommendation without inventing data.
9. Use optional AI drafting support only with approved fictional/sanitized inputs, validate all outputs and explain tool limitations.
10. Execute a coherent fictional client-day simulation and submit checkable artifacts, not merely a written reflection.

## Learning architecture

Each lesson should have **one assessable action**, not just a topic title. Its standard sequence:

| Component | Minimum authoring requirement | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outcome and context | One measurable learner action, why it matters and what permissions are assumed | Learner knows the desired work result |
| Beginner explanation | Terms, input/output, safe decision rule, common errors and a step-by-step written procedure | Can explain the task |
| Demonstration | Original annotated diagram and a scripted walkthrough using fictional data; where relevant, current test-account screenshots/video with text alternatives | Can see the process |
| Worked example | Show a real-looking input, each choice and the checked final work product | Can compare good and bad reasoning |
| Guided task | Partial scaffold, explicit pauses/questions and hints that don't give the answer away | Produces a first attempt |
| Independent task | Different fictional records and instructions; templates downloadable as CSV or accessible text | Produces learner-owned artifact |
| Quality and recovery | Point-by-point checklist, error patterns, feedback, redo pathway and one transfer question | Can improve independently |
| Competence check | Evaluate evidence, not reading time or minimum character count alone | Criterion-based result |

**Content bar:** No lesson ships with a placeholder demonstration, a practice question with no data, a copyable template with no example, or a 'done' state that implies skill verification without reviewing the work. Aim for at least 40% of active learning time on demonstrations, decisions and hands-on work (subject to actual learner pilot results). Do not pad word counts or force arbitrary reading durations.

### Lesson formats

- **Teaching unit:** usually 45–60 minutes of combined instruction and practice, broken into 5–15-minute sections.
- **Deep-work lab:** typically 75–150 minutes with a pause/resume point and a checkable artifact. The first pilot lesson is a 150-minute practice lab with verifiable source records.
- **Integration unit:** 60–90 minutes involving multiple earlier skills, a client brief and a clear handoff.
- Every module closes with a compact portfolio-ready fictional work sample. Clearly label practice work as simulated and not paid employment.

## Detailed module and lesson specifications

### M01 — VA role, trust and operating boundaries (180 min)

**Module decision skill:** Understand which work a VA can execute, which facts need checking and which decisions require a client or authorized professional.

**M01-L01 · From task to outcome · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** translate a client instruction into a measurable result and completion evidence. **Teach/model:** task vs responsibility vs outcome, example of “handle tomorrow's meeting” rewritten with confirmed inputs and a definition of done; explain where ownership ends. **Do:** extract tasks, inputs and unresolved decisions from a four-sentence brief. **Evidence:** a five-row task/outcome/owner/acceptance map.

**M01-L02 · Working agreements and remote readiness · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** set work hours, communication channels, availability, response rules and backup contact arrangements without promising 24/7 service. **Teach/model:** an annotated first-week operating agreement, notification fatigue, minimum viable work setup and connectivity contingency. **Do:** compose a first-week availability and escalation plan from a fictional client policy. **Evidence:** an operating agreement and coverage checklist.

**M01-L03 · Confidentiality, access and authorization · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** classify an access or data-sharing request as allowed, needs approval or prohibited. **Teach/model:** least privilege, password manager sharing, MFA, handling screenshots, portfolio consent and when to stop. **Do:** classify six fictional permission requests. **Evidence:** a reasoned access matrix with safe next actions.

**M01-L04 · Clarifying a vague job brief · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** build a decision-ready brief with missing information, dependencies and escalation points. **Teach/model:** annotate a complete brief and a client response that asks the smallest useful questions. **Do:** rewrite a disorganized client chat into a scoped work order. **Evidence:** a one-page brief, approval questions and audit trail.

**Module checkpoint:** review the scope and access charter from all four lessons. Safety-critical error: claiming authority to approve payments, disclose confidential records or make regulated decisions without authorization.

### M02 — Client communication and escalation (180 min)

**Module decision skill:** Communicate what changed, what is blocked, what needs approval and who owns the next action.

**M02-L01 · Concise decision-ready updates · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** write an update that is understandable in under one minute. **Teach/model:** weak vs strong client updates with facts, requested decision, owner and checkpoint; demonstrate proofreading and contextual subject lines. **Do:** rewrite three vague messages. **Evidence:** before/after email drafts.

**M02-L02 · Questions that unlock work · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** distinguish an answerable clarification from an open-ended handoff of responsibility. **Teach/model:** verify the brief and source first, present two safe options, avoid presumptive approvals. **Do:** formulate precise questions about an ambiguous meeting, folder permissions and invoice discrepancy. **Evidence:** three clarification notes with linked evidence.

**M02-L03 · Complaints, mistakes and difficult news · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** acknowledge, contain and escalate errors without excuses or unauthorized promises. **Teach/model:** the report-correct-prevent loop, public vs internal tone, source verification and accountability. **Do:** draft a correction to a fictional missed follow-up. **Evidence:** customer draft and internal incident note.

**M02-L04 · Asynchronous communication and handoffs · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** leave a handoff that another worker can execute without asking for background. **Teach/model:** “done / open / risk / owner / next check” structure, evidence references and time-zone handoff. **Do:** consolidate six fragmented status messages. **Evidence:** client update and shift-end brief.

**Module checkpoint:** reviewer can trace every stated fact to a source and every pending item to an owner. No promises beyond documented policy.

### M03 — Inbox and customer message operations (300 min)

**Module decision skill:** Turn incoming messages into safe, prioritized actions with verifiable ownership and follow-up.

**M03-L01 · Inbox triage and ownership lab · 150 min — PRACTICAL PILOT DRAFT**  
**Outcome:** classify and process 12 fictional incoming messages using real-looking source records and approval boundaries. **Teach/model:** four detailed worked cases plus original desktop/mobile diagrams and a functional offline practice inbox. **Do:** independently inspect evidence across booking, finance, customer, campaign, meeting and jobs records for eight other messages, produce a decision tracker, two reply drafts, escalation, handoff and a weekly status summary. **Evidence:** exported CSV with citations and five checkable artifacts. **Pilot lesson:** [LESSON_03_01_INBOX_TRIAGE.md](./LESSON_03_01_INBOX_TRIAGE.md); [offline inbox simulator](./practice-inbox-lab.html); [assessment rubric](./ASSESSMENT_RUBRIC_AND_FEEDBACK.md).

**M03-L02 · Inbox organization and search · 60 min**  
**Outcome:** find supporting messages and maintain categories/labels/statuses without hiding required follow-ups. **Teach/model:** annotated Gmail/Outlook test-account views, threads, search operators, delegated-access constraints and a simple “waiting for” tracker. **Do:** organize a fictional inbox export; distinguish archive from closure. **Evidence:** categories and a documented waiting-for queue.

**M03-L03 · Customer replies, approvals and commitments · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** draft accurate responses aligned to a supplied fictional policy. **Teach/model:** confirmation without promise, same-day response, refunds/credit boundaries and approval-first send flow. **Do:** draft two responses and an internal reviewer question. **Evidence:** two message drafts and evidence citations.

**M03-L04 · Inbox close-out and exception recovery · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** reconcile open threads against the task board and leave next-shift coverage. **Teach/model:** no silent closure, overdue exception reports, duplicate threads and owner ambiguity. **Do:** audit a simulated end-of-day inbox with conflicting statuses. **Evidence:** exception log, reconciled board and handoff.

**Module checkpoint:** inbox portfolio must show source, priority, reason, next action, owner, deadline and evidence for each action. A skipped security escalation or unauthorized financial action is a critical error.

### M04 — Calendar, time zones and meetings (195 min)

**Module decision skill:** Book the right people at the right local time without making assumptions about dates or time zones.

**M04-L01 · Time zones and daylight-saving traps · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** convert and verify meeting times using named time zones, not fixed offsets when rules may change. **Teach/model:** a Manila-to-Sydney example across two seasons; use a date-aware calendar converter, spell out date and zone. **Do:** resolve five cross-border invites, including one ambiguous “Thursday 10.” **Evidence:** conversion/assumption log and clarification question.

**M04-L02 · Multi-person scheduling and permissions · 60 min**  
**Outcome:** prepare a complete calendar booking record. **Teach/model:** invitees, tentative holds, buffers, resource calendars, private attachments, link permissions and approval before sending. **Do:** schedule three fictional meetings with constraints and one clash. **Evidence:** three meeting specifications and attendee confirmations.

**M04-L03 · Meeting preparation and follow-up · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** create a useful meeting packet and actionable notes without fabricating decisions. **Teach/model:** agenda, prep list, decision vs discussion, action log. **Do:** convert a fictional call transcript into actions. **Evidence:** agenda, meeting notes and task list.

**M04-L04 · Conflicts and incident recovery · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** resolve a double-booking or changed attendee without losing records. **Teach/model:** pause-before-reschedule sequence, impact assessment, controlled communication and updated invitation. **Do:** repair a fictional scheduling incident. **Evidence:** correction log and two drafts.

**Module checkpoint:** date, named zone, source confirmation, attendee permissions and who approved changes are visible.

### M05 — Files, documents and spreadsheets (210 min)

**Module decision skill:** Produce information another person can locate, trust, edit and audit.

**M05-L01 · Shared drives, naming and access hygiene · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** propose a stable folder tree, naming standard and permission review. **Teach/model:** source of truth, version numbers, safe archiving, least-privilege links. **Do:** plan cleanup of “Final”, “Final2” and similarly named fictional files without deleting originals. **Evidence:** folder map, rename proposal, approval queue.

**M05-L02 · Client documents and controlled editing · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** use headings, comments, suggestions and document QA in a reproducible handoff. **Teach/model:** Google Docs/Word review flow, changes vs approvals, accessible headings, link-checking. **Do:** turn a messy brief into a scannable SOP draft. **Evidence:** annotated document and revision log.

**M05-L03 · Spreadsheet essentials for VA work · 60 min**  
**Outcome:** build a structured tracker with dates, owner, status, filters and a simple accurate formula. **Teach/model:** Google Sheets/Excel columns, data types, sort/filter safety and cross-row formulas without hardcoded answers. **Do:** create a 20-row fictional task tracker. **Evidence:** editable CSV/XLSX/Sheet export with a data dictionary.

**M05-L04 · Data quality and reconciliation · 60 min**  
**Outcome:** locate duplicates, malformed dates, conflicting statuses and missing required fields before changing records. **Teach/model:** difference between a suspect match and a verified duplicate; audit trail and reversible corrections. **Do:** review fictional lead records, identify 10 data-quality issues and propose corrective actions. **Evidence:** quality log, before/after export and reviewer decisions.

**Module checkpoint:** no destructive cleanup without identifiable source and approval; corrections must be auditable.

### M06 — Task management and reliable delivery (195 min)

**Module decision skill:** Keep work moving with clear dependencies, priorities, owners and proof of completion.

**M06-L01 · Break a request into action and acceptance · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** turn a multi-part brief into tasks with acceptance criteria. **Teach/model:** result, task, subtask, dependency, blocker and “done” evidence using a generic task board. **Do:** structure a five-step event-prep request. **Evidence:** ordered task list.

**M06-L02 · Priority, effort and capacity · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** prioritize by impact and deadline, not message arrival or seniority alone. **Teach/model:** urgency vs importance, SLA, buffer, estimates and honest escalation when capacity is exceeded. **Do:** create a four-hour shift plan with six requests. **Evidence:** time-blocked plan and explicit trade-offs.

**M06-L03 · Dependencies, statuses and stalled work · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** detect when “in progress” hides an ownerless dependency. **Teach/model:** waiting vs blocked, due dates, reminders and progress updates with links. **Do:** reconcile a task board containing duplicate/stale statuses. **Evidence:** corrected board and exception list.

**M06-L04 · SOPs, recurring tasks and structured handoffs · 60 min**  
**Outcome:** write a runbook another VA can follow without relying on memory. **Teach/model:** trigger, prerequisites, decision points, exceptions, expected output, rollback/approval and review cadence. **Do:** write an SOP for a fictional weekly status-report process. **Evidence:** versioned SOP and test checklist.

**Module checkpoint:** a new reviewer can repeat the process from the evidence pack without inventing an instruction.

### M07 — Research, fact-checking and reporting (180 min)

**Module decision skill:** Turn uncertain information into a verifiable client update.

**M07-L01 · Research brief and source hierarchy · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** define the question, geography, date, source hierarchy and exclusions. **Teach/model:** official documentation vs marketing claims, freshness and source conflicts. **Do:** plan a fictional supplier comparison. **Evidence:** research brief and source log.

**M07-L02 · Comparison sheets and decision criteria · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** compare several options on consistent, client-approved criteria. **Teach/model:** normalize units, check prices/dates, tag unavailable facts, avoid inventing quotes. **Do:** build a supplier matrix from a provided fictional evidence pack. **Evidence:** transparent comparison with “unknown” values.

**M07-L03 · Verify claims and spot unsupported conclusions · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** identify what a source establishes and what remains an assumption. **Teach/model:** quote-to-claim checks, secondary-source corroboration, screenshot audit and revision notes. **Do:** repair a fictional AI-generated report containing five weak claims. **Evidence:** corrected fact-check log.

**M07-L04 · Client-ready reporting · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** summarize findings, limits, options and requested decisions in plain English. **Teach/model:** one-page executive summary with attached evidence. **Do:** write a concise recommendation from M07-L02 data. **Evidence:** client report and attachments list.

**Module checkpoint:** every factual recommendation has a source; no unsupported “best” or invented performance claim.

### M08 — Responsible AI and data stewardship (180 min)

**Module decision skill:** Use technology as optional drafting help without losing accountability or privacy.

**M08-L01 · Choose safe and unsafe AI uses · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** separate brainstorming, formatting and public-information support from sensitive data or decisions. **Teach/model:** approved tools, access review, data minimization, confidentiality, consent and client-specific policies. **Do:** classify eight fictional AI requests. **Evidence:** allowed/approval/prohibited matrix.

**M08-L02 · Prompting for useful first drafts · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** produce a constrained draft from a sanitized fictional brief. **Teach/model:** inputs, output format, constraints, examples, and why an AI answer is not verified. **Do:** design prompts for two fictional client tasks. **Evidence:** prompt, output and revision log.

**M08-L03 · Verification and hallucination checks · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** evaluate AI output against known evidence. **Teach/model:** source-by-source verification, omitted exceptions, invented figures and tool limitations. **Do:** correct a drafted customer summary with false claims. **Evidence:** error list and revised answer.

**M08-L04 · Disclosure, records and human review · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** describe how AI assistance was used without overstating authorship or accuracy. **Teach/model:** permission, client disclosure where required, redaction limits and what to retain. **Do:** produce a review-ready AI-use note and a non-AI alternative. **Evidence:** audit note, approval request and final reviewed copy.

**Module checkpoint:** the work must remain possible without live AI; no real client information enters unapproved providers.

### M09 — Working from briefs and complex client workflows (210 min)

**Module decision skill:** Run a small end-to-end administrative process through exceptions and handoffs.

**M09-L01 · Intake, instruction and SOP interpretation · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** convert a supplied SOP into a checklist without contradicting it. **Teach/model:** sources of truth, role permissions, exceptions and clarification log. **Do:** reconstruct a fictional new-client intake. **Evidence:** intake checklist and unanswered questions.

**M09-L02 · Risk, exception and escalation routing · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** flag payment, identity, privacy, deadline and customer-impact risks before action. **Teach/model:** incident categories, urgency, documentation and confirmation from authorized staff. **Do:** triage a mixed six-issue queue. **Evidence:** exception matrix and escalation draft.

**M09-L03 · Simulated service desk shift · 60 min**  
**Outcome:** coordinate inbox, scheduling, task board and record updates as a single workflow. **Teach/model:** one connected example and expected handoff structure. **Do:** run a timed, fictional two-hour work queue with interruptions. **Evidence:** integrated workbook with timestamps and links.

**M09-L04 · Process improvement and client reporting · 60 min**  
**Outcome:** distinguish a one-off error from an SOP control gap. **Teach/model:** root cause without blame, measurable acceptance, owner and safe rollback. **Do:** propose a workflow change from M09-L03's audit trail. **Evidence:** change request and concise stakeholder report.

**Module checkpoint:** learner does not fix missing information by guessing, modifying unauthorized records or closing unresolved work.

### M10 — Integrated simulation and practical assessment (285 min)

**Module decision skill:** Independently manage a fictional client-day with evidence and safe escalation.

**M10-L01 · Capstone brief and controls · 45 min**  
**Outcome:** restate the client goal, identify authorized tasks, deadlines, documents and gaps. **Teach/model:** annotated sample brief and deliverables register; explain acceptable simulation assumptions. **Do:** prepare an authority and dependency map. **Evidence:** capstone work plan.

**M10-L02 · Guided mini-simulation · 60 min**  
**Outcome:** transfer skills across inbox, calendar, tracker and customer updates without additional teaching prompts. **Teach/model:** instructor works through a different fictional case, then reveals the review criteria. **Do:** complete a six-event practice queue. **Evidence:** mini-project and reflection.

**M10-L03 · Independent capstone — operating shift · 90 min**  
**Outcome:** execute one multi-channel 12-event shift in the supplied fictional client case; stop at approval boundaries. **Do:** build and update the inbox board, task sheet, calendar spec, file/data QA log and decision record. **Evidence:** editable working files plus evidence references.

**M10-L04 · Independent capstone — client handoff and quality review · 90 min**  
**Outcome:** make the work inspectable by a second VA or a hiring manager. **Do:** draft two stakeholder messages, reconcile statuses, create the end-of-shift report, complete self-QA and submit for criterion-based review. **Evidence:** completed portfolio, reviewed rubric and a targeted correction if needed.

**Module checkpoint:** separate knowledge-check completion from practical competency review. A certificate for V2 practical proficiency is not issued merely for opening lessons or selecting quiz answers.

## Final capstone: fictional client operations simulation

### Setting

**Client:** Harbour Lane Services, a fictional Sydney small-business service team. Learner works as a remote administrative assistant. **No real customers or active systems are involved.** Case pack is dated and carries explicit time zones, internal permissions, a support-response policy and a delegated-authority matrix.

### Evidence pack required from curriculum author

- Client brief (operating hours, role limits, approval contacts, holiday/coverage exceptions).
- 12 inbound messages across customer, operations, finance, security and supplier work.
- Calendar requests with at least two different named time zones and one ambiguous date.
- 15-row task board containing conflicts, duplicates, dependencies and an overdue item.
- 20-row spreadsheet with intentional data-quality issues and a trustworthy source register.
- Four sample documents with duplicate names, outdated versions or unclear access.
- One SOP covering a recurring business process, with one controlled exception.
- Distinct expected-evidence checklist and assessor guide stored in **restricted** reviewer space when it contains grading keys, not a public course repo.

### Required learner submission

1. Scope and delegation map identifying actions allowed, on hold or delegated.
2. Prioritized inbox/action tracker with cited messages, time stamps, owners and due dates.
3. Two customer or stakeholder message drafts with verified facts and no unauthorized promises.
4. Meeting booking specification showing date, time-zone source, attendees, constraints and approval.
5. Task board plus a spreadsheet quality log; proposed corrections must be reversible.
6. Folder/file disposition plan rather than deletions based on filenames alone.
7. Two-paragraph researched/verified update with a source reference list.
8. One exception/escalation record for a safety- or finance-sensitive decision.
9. Clear end-of-day handoff with what is done, open, blocked, next owner and next checkpoint.
10. Signed learner declaration that the materials are simulated and any tool assistance was disclosed under the training policy.

### Capstone rubric — 100 points

| Dimension | Weight | What the reviewer must actually check |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Task execution and accuracy | 25 | Correct transformations, statuses, source-linked details and realistic deliverables |
| Ownership, priority and timeliness | 20 | Consequence-based order, correct dates, named owners and workable handoff checkpoints |
| Client communications | 15 | Clear, accurate, appropriately toned drafts; decision/approval requests are actionable |
| Calendar and timezone execution | 10 | Correct date-aware zones, meeting constraints and source/approval records |
| Records and data quality | 15 | Duplicate detection, reversible edits, privacy/security of files and evidence trail |
| Research, judgment and integrity | 15 | Correctly documented limits, supported claims, safe AI decisions and escalation |
| **Total** | **100** | |

**Proposed outcome:** 80/100 or above **and** no unresolved critical safety error. A critical failure includes exposing genuine confidential information, treating an unsolicited credential reset as safe, approving payment or a refund without authority, fabricating completion evidence or making a regulated decision. These require correction and reassessment regardless of numeric score.

**Reviewer procedure:** mark the actual submitted files against criterion-level anchors (complete/partial/missing); identify the exact line or row to repair; give one clear next attempt pathway. Use double review on a sample of submissions to calibrate consistency. Learners can request a second review. Publishing a V2 credential requires an intentionally designed reviewed-practical record, not merely the current automated lesson/quiz completion state.

## Design and production quality standards

**Instructional quality**

- Each lesson teaches both the normal path and at least one failure/exception path.
- Every multi-step workflow includes the starting input, each decision and a completed example output.
- Avoid repeatedly teaching the same generic “escalate to your manager” message instead of the actual job-specific workflow.
- Place an authentic downloadable resource and task instructions before the independent assignment.
- Practical work uses invented clients, synthetic dates/data, no real passwords, patient information or live transaction activity.
- Build text alternatives, captions/transcripts, accessible table headers, keyboard controls and non-color-only status cues.
- Support learners on mobile for reading; where a spreadsheet/desktop is meaningfully necessary, say so and provide accessible alternative ways to practice.
- Add self-check questions that require reasoning. Do not mistake scroll %, active time, minimum text length or quiz score for demonstrated skill.
- Keep assessment evidence and criterion scores visible to learner; allow retry after remediation. Prevent public access to any hidden grading key.

**Software demonstration rules**

- The curriculum author should provide an original generic workflow diagram and test-account storyboard first.
- A product-specific walkthrough must name the product/version/date tested; use permissioned captures from a dummy account. Avoid altered or invented screenshots that look like current live Gmail, Outlook, Xero or other proprietary app UI.
- Do not teach credentials sharing, executing real payments/refunds, unapproved bulk-email sends or live patient-record operations.
- Build equivalent learner paths for common software ecosystems where possible and label differences clearly.
- Keep tool features and local regulatory details under scheduled content review, not permanent static promises.

**Learning analytics, with restraint**

Track voluntary opt-in learning data sufficient to evaluate teaching quality, not personal customer information: started, practice opened, practice submitted, rubric criterion results, retries, time-to-completion band, abandon point and learner feedback. Report aggregated cohort results. **Do not** turn exercise drafts into recruiter/hiring signals without a separately designed, lawful and transparent review process.

### Suggested pilot acceptance criteria (targets to validate, not facts)

- Recruit **6–10 novices** and **2 working VAs/trainers** representing different software familiarity.
- At least 80% of pilot participants can find the lesson instructions, start the CSV task and resume after a break without moderator intervention.
- At least 70% of novices can produce a passing inbox triage artifact **after feedback and a supported retry**.
- Zero critical authorization/security mistakes are left uncorrected in the final pilot submissions.
- Two assessors agree within 10 points on at least 80% of double-marked example submissions; revise ambiguous rubric language.
- Identify confusing terms, reading load, device blockers and lessons that run much longer than planned before committing the rest of the curriculum.

These are **proposed pilot gates**, not claims of current learner achievement.

### Release phases

**Phase A — curriculum and pilot (this branch):** approve blueprint, write exemplar lesson, test with real novices in a safe fictional environment, revise authoring standards.

**Phase B — remaining authoring:** produce lessons in module order, case packs, original diagrams, worked examples, templates, proper assessment prompts and reviewer rubrics; QA every lesson for factual accuracy and tool freshness.

**Phase C — V2 platform build:** implement accessible save/resume, versioned enrollment, file/sheet evidence, safer retention, human/rubric assessment, remediation, reviewer access, review audit logs and optional certificate issuance. No automatic recruiting/hiring integration.

**Phase D — staged launch:** role-gated preview, accessibility/security review, data protection review, migration verification, full browser test and deliberate activation after stakeholder sign-off.

### Done means

A release is **not done** because 40 titles exist or the copy is long. It is done when beginners can follow the steps, produce inspectable work, receive useful corrections and complete a coherent simulated client-day without crossing their authority.
